From IIT Bombay to East Lansing — a journey of uncommon will

The Bridge Between Worlds

In the 1960s, a young engineer named Jitendra Saxena left India's most elite engineering program — IIT Bombay — and chose Michigan State University to continue his education. It was not an accident. MSU had built something extraordinary: the nation's first Office of International Programs, world-class engineering labs fueled by the Space Race, and a culture of computing innovation born from MISTIC, one of America's earliest university computers.

Jit carried MSU's "Spartan Engineer" mindset into a career that would produce Netezza — acquired by IBM — and transform the global data industry. Now, decades later, his gift returns that spirit to its source, fueling the DeepTech Lab and a new generation of Spartan innovators who will build technology at the precipice of the possible.

The bridge between a newly independent India and the American Midwest produced a titan of industry. Today, that same bridge carries innovation back to MSU.

Pillars of Impact

The Saxena gift advances MSU's mission across three transformative pillars.

💡

Innovation & Deep Technology

Advancing AI-driven diagnostics, autonomous vehicles, secure edge computing, and resource-efficient machine learning — technologies that were impossible yesterday and may become pervasive tomorrow.

🎓

Student Impact & Talent Pipeline

Training the next generation of engineers and entrepreneurs who build and validate systems on physical platforms, connecting technical ideas to real-world user needs and industry problems.

🏭

Commercialization & Ventures

Moving research beyond publication toward market readiness — through customer discovery, stakeholder engagement, NSF I-Corps training, and real-world deployment of deep-tech innovations.
